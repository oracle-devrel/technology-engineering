Good morning, and thank you for joining the operations review for the third quarter of 2026. I will cover volumes, service levels, costs and the outlook for Q4, and then we will open the floor for questions. The headline is that total shipments reached 4.82 million units, up 11.3 percent on the same quarter last year and 2.7 percent ahead of the plan we agreed in June. On-time delivery finished at 96.4 percent, against a target of 95 percent, and the damage rate fell to 0.31 percent, the lowest figure since we began tracking it in 2019.

Let me take the regions in turn. North America shipped 2.14 million units at an average cost of 3 dollars and 12 cents per unit, which is 8 cents better than Q2. Europe shipped 1.57 million units, with the Rotterdam and Hamburg hubs each handling more than 400,000 units for the first time. Asia Pacific came in at 1.11 million units, held back by the typhoon closures in late August that cost us roughly 14 operating days across the Taiwan and Philippines sites. Latin America remains small at 2 percent of volume but grew 38 percent year on year, driven almost entirely by the Mexico City facility that opened on March 15.

Service levels were strong across the board. First-contact resolution reached 83 percent, average handle time dropped to 6 minutes 40 seconds, and the net promoter score rose 5 points to 47. The one exception is returns processing, where the median time from receipt to refund rose from 2.1 days to 3.4 days. The cause is known. The new scanning system went live on July 7 and the integration with the refund module was delayed until August 22, so the backlog built for six weeks before clearing. We expect the metric to return below 2.5 days in October.

On costs, total operating expense was 61.4 million dollars, or 12 dollars and 74 cents per unit, which is 4.1 percent below plan. Fuel accounted for 9.8 million, labour for 27.3 million, facilities for 11.2 million and technology for 6.9 million, with the balance in insurance, packaging and miscellaneous items. Headcount ended the quarter at 3,412 full-time equivalents, 86 lower than June, mainly through natural attrition at the two sites we are consolidating in Ohio. Overtime hours fell 17 percent, which is the clearest sign that the scheduling changes introduced in May are working as intended.

Looking ahead, we are planning for peak volumes of between 1.9 and 2.2 million units in November alone, which would be a record. Temporary hiring begins on October 6 with a target of 540 seasonal staff. We have secured an additional 12 percent of line-haul capacity on contracts fixed through January 31, and we have pre-positioned 3.5 million units of packaging in the regional hubs. The risks are the usual ones: weather, carrier reliability and the possibility that the port labour negotiations on the West Coast are not settled before the end of the month. We will send a written update on all three by Friday, October 17. That concludes the prepared remarks.
